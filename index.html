AI adoption is outpacing educational infrastructure

Artificial intelligence is reshaping work, education, and organizational practice. Yet the systems needed to prepare people to adopt AI effectively, safely, responsibly, and sustainably have not developed at the same pace. Competency definitions remain fragmented across educational levels and occupational contexts, measurement is uneven, and institutions often lack practical models for translating AI strategy into curriculum, assessment, and educator practice. Nexus Institute exists to help close that gap.

The AI Education and Workforce Readiness Initiative

Five interconnected systems, developed as a single body of work to define and validate AI competencies, develop pathways for building them, measure their attainment, support their implementation across education and workforce settings, and govern how AI is integrated into learning systems.

01
Define

AI Competency Frameworks and Standards

Defines the knowledge, skills, metacognition, and judgment learners and workers need for effective, safe, responsible, sustainable AI adoption — across STEM and non-STEM fields and occupations alike.

02
Develop

Competency-Based Learning and Credentialing Systems

Turns defined competencies into structured learning experiences, authentic assessment, and stackable credentials that institutions can adopt directly.

03
Measure

Assessment and Measurement Systems

The Generic AI Competency (GAIC) Scale is being developed and psychometrically validated to measure whether AI competency is actually developing, not simply whether a course was completed.

Validation underway
04
Implement

Global AI Education and Workforce Readiness Implementation Framework (GAEWRIF)

Explains how an institution builds the organizational capacity, leadership, governance, educator support, to sustain AI adoption at scale. Designed for adaptation across institutional and, over time, national contexts.

In active development
05
Govern

Human–AI Instructional Design Operating System (IDOS)

An enterprise operating framework, introduced in The Learning Operating System, for designing, producing, and continuously improving the Institute's AI-assisted instructional materials under continuous human oversight and review.

Featured research instrument

Generic AI Competency (GAIC) Scale

A discipline-agnostic measure of applied AI competency across cognitive, affective, and technomoral dimensions — designed to work across STEM and non-STEM disciplines rather than within a single field.

Validation underway

Research → Implementation

Nexus connects evidence generation with institutional application. We develop, measure, test, refine, and disseminate educational infrastructure rather than treating research and implementation as separate activities.

Research program

AI competency measurement

Developing and validating instruments, including the GAIC Scale, that measure AI competency across educational and workforce contexts.

Competency-based learning & credentialing

Studying how competency frameworks translate into learning, assessment, credentialing, and workforce preparation, including a national labor-market study mapping employer demand for AI competencies.

Institutional AI capability

Studying how organizations translate AI strategy into curriculum, governance, educator practice, and sustainable implementation, through GAEWRIF and related scholarship.

Start where the challenge is greatest.

Nexus Institute for AI begins its implementation work in underserved and Appalachian communities in Ohio, designing educational infrastructure for the conditions in which AI adoption can be most difficult. Evidence generated in these contexts can then inform adaptation across broader institutional and workforce settings. Ohio is not the limit of the mission — it's the starting point.

About Nexus

Nexus Institute for AI Learning, or NIAIL, is an independent nonprofit research and implementation organization focused on the educational infrastructure required for responsible AI adoption.

Founder

NIAIL was founded by Joshua Osondu, a Ph.D. Candidate in Innovative Learning Design and Technology at Ohio University. His research focuses on AI competency, educational measurement, instructional systems design, implementation science, and AI governance.

Platform

The Institute provides an independent organizational platform for continuing this work beyond any single research project, institution, or appointment — developing, evaluating, and disseminating these tools for use across the field.
